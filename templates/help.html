Welcome to the Music Room

This isn't a spreadsheet for your records. It's a place you walk into. Here's the whole tour — every room, every tool, every little button — in plain language. Skim the rail on the left, or just scroll. Nothing here can break anything; poke at everything.

Your files, your data

What Music Room will and won't do

You're trusting this thing with a record collection, so here it is, plainly:

  • Importing only reads. Your files stay exactly where they are. Nothing is moved, renamed or written into.
  • Organising is optional and explicit. If you ask for it, tidied copies are filed as Music Room Library / A / Artist / Album / 01 - Artist - Title.flac — copy by default, move only if you choose it, and it never overwrites: a clashing name gets a “ (2)”.
  • Everything it learns lives in its own database on your machine — analysis never writes into your audio files, and writing tags into a file is always a button you press.
  • Nothing goes to a cloud. There's no account, no online library, no external database of you. The app fetches things from free open sources — cover art and metadata from iTunes / MusicBrainz / Discogs / Deezer, and the posts from feeds you follow in Explore — but your music, your notes and your listening stay here. (In a tester build, the one thing that leaves is feedback you choose to send with the feedback button.)
  • There is no AI in this app. None. The "For you" feed in Explore is plain word-matching against interests you typed yourself — you can read exactly how it ranks things in the Explore section below.
  • Nothing is hard-deleted. Removing a file sends it to the Trash or a recoverable folder, never oblivion.
Back up anyway. Music Room looks after its own database (it keeps copies in your backup folder, and on your music drive if you use one). Your music files are yours to back up — Time Machine, or a plain copy onto a second drive. Do it. Always.

The metaphor

The big idea

Music Room is built like a place, not a database. When you arrive you're in The Forest — a map. Dotted around it are places: rooms, gigs, moods, moments. "Sunset Beach." "The 4am Warehouse." "Sunday Kitchen." You make these up; they're how you think about where music gets played.

One place is special: the cabin in the middle — the Music Room itself. Every track you own lives there. The rest of the forest is where things belong: a track can sit in the Music Room and in "Sunset Beach" and in "Closing Time" all at once. You're not filing things into one folder — you're noticing where they fit.

The whole app is one loop:

Home

The Forest

The map. Trees, a clearing, your places scattered through it, and the Music Room cabin at the centre.

  • Drag a place to move it around the forest — its position is remembered.
  • Click a place to walk into it.
  • Hover a place → the ⚙ cog lets you edit it without leaving the map.
  • Scroll / pinch to zoom; drag empty space to pan; the ⊙ button re-centres on the Music Room.
  • + make a new place (bottom-right) starts a new room.

The light changes with the actual time of day where you are — morning is bright, late night goes deep and blue. That's not decoration you control; it's the forest breathing.

Places

The Music Room & your places

The Music Room = everything. You can't delete it. Every imported track starts here.

Other places are yours to invent. Give one a name, a vibe, a time of night, a crowd, a colour — then put records in it. A track can live in as many places as you like; putting it in "Sunset Beach" doesn't take it out of the Music Room. Nothing on disk ever moves — places are how you see the collection, not folders.

Putting records in a place

  • Right-click any record in the room → add to a place.
  • Or open a record → in its panel, under In the forest, use + Add to another place…
  • Or fill the □ box as you browse and drop the lot somewhere at once.

Browsing

The Music Room floor

Walk into any place and its records fill the room. The bar along the bottom is the whole language of the floor:

  • view — Cloud floats every cover in space: drag to look around, two fingers to glide, pinch to zoom. Grid lines them up: the slider sets the size, and at 1 a single record fills the screen.
  • arrange — BPM, Key, Year or Colour. In the cloud each becomes a wall you can walk along; in the grid, a sort (press again to flip the direction). Press the lit one once more for the plain cloud.
  • tools — ◈ gather pulls a filtered set into one tight cluster; ✩ presets saves filter combinations; ⌬ structures lets you place your own tags in the room and watch the records gather into your shape.

Up top: ⌕ searches title and artist as you type; ⊞ filters by your tags, genres, BPM, key, year and file type — they stack. Once the room is narrowed, buttons appear beside the count: ＋ cue all, ▶ play all, ＋ tag all (pick several tags, press once), and make a set (names it, fills it in this order, takes you there).

  • Click a record — its full panel opens (next section).
  • +▶ on a card — quietly queues it on the player. Nothing ever autoplays.
  • □ on a card — drops it in the box, a little crate you carry while you dig. Open the box from the Tools bag; everything in it can be cued, tagged or made into a set in one go.
  • Right-click a record — the quick menu: cue, play, open, box, export.
Records whose files are on an unplugged drive rest quietly (a pill tells you how many). Plug the drive in and they wake without a reload.
One track, up close

The track panel

Click any track and a panel slides in. Top to bottom:

  • Player — play / pause, scrub, volume. ▶ auto-play can be toggled. → player hands the track to the persistent player so it keeps going while you browse; ＋ cue lines it up there without starting it. ⊙ stamp pins the current time into a note — timestamped notes become rekordbox memory cues when you export.
  • File — format, size, bitrate, the path on disk. The ▶ reveals it; the 🗑 removes that file version. Got both an MP3 and a FLAC of the same track? They live here as versions, and your notes stick to the track, not the file — upgrade to FLAC and you keep everything.
  • Analysis — BPM and key (with the Camelot code). The ✓ marks a value as verified; the ✎ lets you correct it by hand.
  • Sound — your ears, drawn as bars. (Its own section below.)
  • Genres / Tags — label the music. (Own section below.)
  • In the forest — which places this track lives in; add it to more.
  • In sets — which sets it's part of; + Add to a set… drops it onto the end of one.
  • Notes — write anything; timestamp it with stamp; record a voice note. This is Deep Listening.
  • Label — the record label. The ↻ look up button asks MusicBrainz / Discogs / Deezer; + self released is one click.
  • "Only here" — a quiet badge if a track isn't on the streaming services, so you know what's genuinely yours.
  • Remove / Delete (bottom) — Remove keeps the file on disk (parks it in your "Removed" folder, skipped on future imports); Delete sends it to Trash.
The music never stops

The player

The little cassette bar is the app's one continuous player. It lives in the app itself, not in any page — so you can wander anywhere and the music carries on. Everything that plays, plays through it.

  • ＋ cue adds a record to the queue without interrupting anything; ▶ play plays it now. Nothing ever autoplays uninvited.
  • The queue — click the ♫ count to see it; drag rows to reorder, click a row's title to open that record, ⤴ plays one next.
  • – folds the bar to a small ♫ tab; ✕ puts it away (the music pauses, the queue is kept). Cueing something brings it back.
  • It remembers. Quit the app mid-queue and next launch the queue is still there, folded quietly into the tab — press play and carry on.
  • Drag it anywhere on the screen — it stays where you put it.
  • If a record can't play (file missing, drive unplugged), the player says so briefly and moves on — never a silent skip.
What it sounds like

Sound & analysis

Run analysis (on import, or later from Tools → Analyse) and Music Room listens to each track and draws three sliders plus a six-band fingerprint.

The three sliders

  • Loudness — how loud, measured properly (not just the peak). Handy for matching levels.
  • Dynamic range — narrow (squashed/loud-all-through) vs wide (breathes, quiet bits and loud bits).
  • Brightness — how much energy sits up top. This is the cumulative high end (Presence + Brilliance together), so it matches what your ears call "bright."

Each slider is shown relative to your whole library — "brighter than 80% of your library," that kind of thing. It's about where this track sits among your records, not an absolute number.

The six bands

The little bar chart breaks the sound into frequency ranges, each labelled with its Hz:

  • Sub 20–60 Hz · Bass 60–250 Hz · Lo-mid 250–500 Hz
  • Body 0.5–2 kHz · Presence 2–5 kHz · Brilliance 5–11 kHz

Tall bar = lots of energy there, relative to your library. A bass-heavy dub plate spikes on the left; a crisp, airy track lights up the right. Hover any bar for the exact dB and percentile.

Labelling

Genres, tags & ✦ Suggest

Genres are the "what is this music" label; tags are your own free words ("peak time", "heartbreak", "for the smoking area"). Both autocomplete from what you've used before, and edits show up everywhere that track appears.

Not sure what something is? Hit ✦ Suggest.

In the Genres section, the ✦ Suggest button asks the internet — Discogs, MusicBrainz, Deezer, iTunes (and Last.fm if you add a key) — what they think the track is. You get back a row of suggestion chips, each tagged with where it came from (D Discogs, MB MusicBrainz, Dz Deezer, iT iTunes, L Last.fm). Click any chip to keep it. Nothing is added automatically — you're always the one deciding.

Discogs gives the best DJ-grade sub-genres, so a Discogs token in Settings makes Suggest much sharper. It's free and there's a link in Settings.
Your toolkit

The Tools — the bag you carry

Bottom-right of the room. Tap it to open your tools:

  • Box — the crate. Drop records in with the □ on any card (or right-click → into the box), and it follows you as you browse. Open it and the room shows just what's inside — cue it all, tag it all, or make a set of it in one press.
  • Deep Listening — sit with one record and write. Notes are stamped to the second as the track plays; press 1–0 for your own quick categories. Timestamped notes become rekordbox memory cues when you export.
  • Player — brings the cassette back if you've put it away.
  • BPM Tapper — tap along to anything, get its tempo.
  • ✦ make this place yours — the atmosphere panel: mood, tint, ambient sound, backdrop, live in the room around you.

Make it yours

Making a place feel like a place

Every place can look completely different — that's the point. Open a place, hit the ⚙ next to its name (or hover it in the forest), and find the Atmosphere section:

  • Mood — a named preset (Peat Fire, Midnight Rave, Sea Cave, Candlelit Pub…) that sets a whole look in one click. Then tweak whatever you want on top.
  • Ground colour — the base colour of the room. The whole place re-skins around it — panels, text, accents all derive from it automatically and stay readable.
  • Fonts — pick a title font and a body font (insular, classical, old-book, clean…).
  • Illustration — every place has a little hand-drawn icon (a pub, a warehouse, a beach, a cave…). You can pick which one, and recolour it with four roles — Light (windows / fire glow), Structure (the main mass), Outline, and Detail — with a live preview as you go.
  • Background image — drop in a photo as a soft backdrop.
  • Ambient sound — a quiet loop that belongs to the place (rain, room tone, a field recording). Toggle it with the ♪ button bottom-left. It plays under your music, not instead of it.

(The Music Room's cabin is always a cabin — you can recolour it, but its shape is fixed, because it's the well.)

Building sets

Sets

A set is a named, ordered sequence of records. There are two kinds, and the app asks which when you make one:

  • Charted — a programmed set: ordered, with a transition note between each pair of records. For planning a journey start to finish.
  • Set prep — a simple ordered list to pull from in the booth. This is the one most DJs live in.

Filling one

  • In the room: narrow to what you want (search, filters, the box) and press make a set — everything in hand joins in the order shown, and the set opens.
  • One at a time: a record's panel has + Add to a set…
  • Inside a set: drag records into order; play it through; write the transitions (Charted).

Every set-prep becomes a playlist in rekordbox when you export. Build here, play anywhere.

Looking outward

Explore

The reading room — new music and writing from sources you choose, without opening Instagram. Two views: the Stream (one thing at a time, ←/→ to flick, the strip along the bottom shows what's coming) and the Wall (everything at once).

  • ✦ sources — the publications, stations and labels you follow. Add from the catalogue (search a vibe, not just a name), or paste any site's link and Explore finds its feed. Their posts become your stream.
  • ✦ tune — your own algorithm, run by you, readable: write interests in your own words, give each a weight. An item's score is the sum of the interests whose words appear in its title, summary or source — whole words, no model, no learning, no black box. A dash of wander throws in the occasional off-piste find on purpose. A source you added in the last fortnight gets its newest posts onto the first page regardless, so a new voice is always visible.
  • ♥ wishlist — the records you're hunting, sorted into three honest boxes: I need this now!, I'll have another listen, not sure. Paste a link and it fills in the details. Buying always routes OUT to where the artist sells — there's no store in here.
  • ⊞ filters — by type (article / mix / video / podcast), source, or date.

The buttons tell the truth

▶ listen plays a mix or podcast through the player, right here. ▶ watch plays a video in the room. ▤ read in the music room only appears once the app has checked it can really extract that article into its reading view — if an item only offers Open in Browser, that's the honest answer for that page. s saves a piece into Listen; w wishlists; x lets one go (with an undo).

Your ears, logged

Listen

Everything you've written while listening — every Deep Listening note, every voice memo — gathered in one place and filterable to the hilt: by text, tag, genre, place, when you wrote it, typed vs voice. It's the diary of how you actually hear your records over time.

Bringing music in

Import

  • Point it at a folder — a whole drive is fine. Import reads your files; it never changes them. Anything it's seen before (even a copy with a different name) is recognised and skipped, so re-importing is always safe.
  • Where files end up is your choice — leave them where they are (nothing on disk changes), or copy / move them into your Music Room Library folder, filed alphabetically. Never overwritten — a clashing name gets a "(2)".
  • Also analyse reads BPM, key and the sound fingerprint on the way in. It's the one heavy job in the app — a big first import works the laptop hard and that's normal. Let it finish; don't quit the app mid-import.
  • Undo last import — one button puts everything back if a run went wrong.
Sending music out

Export & rekordbox

The round trip, in one picture:

  • Update rekordbox XML writes your whole collection in one go. Every place, structure and set-prep arrives in rekordbox as a playlist; your genres land in its Genre field, your tags in Comments. In rekordbox, point its rekordbox xml setting at the file once and refresh after each export.
  • BPM and key are deliberately NOT exported — rekordbox analyses fresh and is better at its own beat-grid. Its results can come back the other way: Tools → Analyse → Import rekordbox analysis.
  • Cues, if you want them — tick the box and your timestamped notes become memory cues. New tracks only is the safe one: it never touches cues you've adjusted in rekordbox since.
  • Export a folder — any place, set or selection can also become a plain folder of files (plus a playlist file), for a USB stick or anywhere else.
  • If records would be skipped because their drive isn't plugged in, the page warns you loudly first.
The workshop

Tools

  • Import / Export — the two doors; each has its own section above.
  • Analyse — run (or top up) the heavy lifting across the whole library: BPM/key, the sound fingerprint, artwork, labels. Run all fires everything still missing; leave it overnight for a big library.
  • The Map — your collection on a globe, by where the artists are from.
  • Insights — what you play, what leads you to play, which artists you've supported with real money. All of it stays on this computer.
  • Studio — link your own productions (Ableton) into the library.
  • Recordings — capture what the laptop is playing (a mix, a stream) and keep it as its own thing, outside the record collection.
  • BPM Tapper — tap along, get a tempo.
  • Fix — bulk-tidy metadata gaps: titles, artists, years, labels.
  • Audio health — is every file where it should be? Missing and damaged files listed, safe-eject for the drive.
  • Removed — records you've removed are parked here, restorable.
  • Spotify — optional: cross-check what you own against your saved tracks, import a playlist as a wishlist.
  • Settings — the next section.
Knobs & paths

Settings — everything machine-specific lives here

This is where you (and any friend running their own copy) wire things up to your own computer:

  • Music library folder — where your audio lives. Pre-fills the Import screen.
  • Where Music Room keeps your data — your database, artwork, voice notes, moodboards and ambient sounds. Back this one folder up and you've backed up everything. Reveal in Finder shows you exactly where it is.
  • Preferred browser — which browser external links open in.
  • Discogs token — free; sharpens label + genre lookups. (Link to generate one is right there.)
  • Last.fm key — free, optional; adds Last.fm tags to genre suggestions.
  • Pixabay key — free, optional; for browsing photos as place moodboards.
  • Export / Backup / Removed folders — where exports go, where backups copy to, and where "removed" tracks get parked. All have sensible defaults; change them if you like.
  • Deep Listening shortcuts — keyboard shortcuts for note categories.
None of these are required to start except the Music library folder. The API keys are all optional — Music Room works without them, it just knows a little less about your records.

Glossary

    {% if not is_gr %}
  • Place — a room in the forest you invent (a venue, a mood, a night). Records belong to any number of places; files never move.
  • The Music Room — the cabin in the middle: the place that holds every record you own.
    • {% endif %}
  • The box — the crate you fill while browsing (□ on any card) and act on in one go.
  • Set — a named, ordered sequence of records. Charted = programmed with transitions; set prep = an ordered list for the booth. Set-preps become rekordbox playlists on export.
  • Structure — your own tags placed spatially in the room; the records gather into the shape you drew.
  • Tags & genres — genres say what the music is; tags are any words of your own. Both filter everywhere. There are no built-in tags — the vocabulary is entirely yours.
  • Timestamped note — a note pinned to a second of a track (⊙ stamp, or Deep Listening). These become rekordbox memory cues on export.
  • Camelot code — a key written for mixing (8A, 9B…): neighbouring numbers mix cleanly, letter A is minor, B major.
  • Analysis — the app listening to a file to read BPM, key, loudness and the six-band sound fingerprint. Local, never touches the audio file.
  • Wishlist — records you're hunting, in three boxes by feeling. Buying always routes out to where the artist sells.
  • Source — a publication, station or label whose posts feed your Explore stream.
  • Deep Listening — the sit-with-one-record mode: timestamped notes as it plays.
