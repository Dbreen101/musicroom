Music Room
Fáilte · Welcome

This is the Music Room

It's not really a library app. It's a place you walk into — a forest, with rooms, and a well at the centre that holds everything.

But a room is a quiet, strange place with nothing in it. The Music Room only comes alive once your records are inside. So before anything else, let's bring some music in — it takes a minute, and you can stop whenever you like.

A quick word on how it treats your music: it reads your files where they already are. Everything it learns goes in its own database, not into your files. The next few steps set that up.

Everything here is optional. Skip any step and the door's still open — you can always come back to this walkthrough from the Help tab.
The essentials get your music in, and each part of the app says hello the first time you open it. The full version shows you around everything first.
Your files, first

Before anything touches your library

First, the ground rules. Your music collection is precious, so here is exactly what Music Room will and won't do with it:

  • When you import, it only reads your files. It never moves them, renames them, or writes anything into them.
  • If you later ask it to organise your library, it makes copies (it can move instead, but only if you tell it to). If two files would end up with the same name, it adds “ (2)” rather than replacing anything.
  • Everything it works out about a track goes in its own database, not into your files. Writing tags into a file is a button you press yourself.
  • Deleting something sends it to the Trash. Nothing just disappears.
Even so: back up your music first. Copy the folder onto another drive, or let Time Machine run. Ten minutes now, and nothing any app does can ever really hurt you.

Bring it in

Load some music

Point it at the folder your music is in right now and press Import. It reads the files where they are and your records appear in the Music Room. One folder now, more whenever you like. Importing again never duplicates anything.

Tip: in Finder, right-click a folder → hold ⌥ Option → “Copy folder as Pathname”, then paste it here.
“Also analyse” works out BPM, key and the sound fingerprint as it goes — slower (5–15s a track), but you can also do it later. Leave it off to import fast.
Tidy up (optional)

Organise into one folder

If your music is scattered across folders, Music Room can sort it into one tidy library — alphabetised by artist:

Music Room Library / A / Aphex Twin / Selected Ambient Works / 01 - Radiator.flac

Only your imported tracks get organised, so do this after importing. Move relocates files and can't be undone automatically — Copy is the safe choice if you're unsure. Either way nothing is ever overwritten: two tracks landing on the same name get a “ (2)”, never a replacement.
Let it listen

Analyse your tracks

Analysis listens to each track, here on your Mac (nothing is uploaded), and works out:

  • BPM & key — for beatmatching and harmonic mixing (the Camelot wheel).
  • Loudness & dynamics — so you can match levels across records.
  • A six-band sound fingerprint — sub / bass / mids / brilliance — so you can see what a track sounds like and find "more like this".

It also fetches cover art and works out each cover's colour. All of this powers the visual tools — the room's layouts, the filters, the colour sort. Everything analysis learns goes into Music Room's own database — it never writes into your audio files.

Big libraries take a while — leave it running, even overnight. You can also do this any time from Tools → Analyse.
The tour · The Music Room

Playing records

  • Click any record and its panel opens. Press ▶ to play it.
  • The little player in the corner keeps going wherever you wander. Change page, browse, read — the music doesn't stop.
  • ＋ cue lines a record up next without interrupting anything. → player hands the record you're looking at over to the player.
  • While something plays, ⊙ stamp pins that exact moment into a note. Handy for marking the bit you love.
  • Tags are whatever words you want. Add them in the panel; each gets a colour, and the search and filters understand them.
The tour · The Forest

Places

  • The forest is your map. The cabin in the middle is the Music Room — every record you import lives in there.
  • Make a place for anything that means something to you. A night, a mood, a set you're building. Click one to walk in.
  • Right-click a record inside any room to add it to a place. A record can belong to many places at once — nothing gets moved or copied.
The tour · Explore

Finding new music

  • Explore is a stream of articles, mixes and videos from sources you choose — magazines, radio stations, labels. You can read and listen without leaving.
  • In ✦ tune, write down what you care about, in your own words. The stream sorts itself around that. No one is watching; it's just word-matching, and you can read exactly how it works in Help.
  • ♥ wishlist holds the records you want. Paste a link to one and it fills in the rest. Buying always goes straight to the artist.
The tour · Sets

Building a set, leaving for the decks

  • There are two kinds of set. A Charted set is sequenced, with transitions. Set prep is a simple ordered list to take to the booth.
  • Add records from anywhere in the app, then drag them into shape.
  • When you're ready for the decks: Tools → Export → Update rekordbox XML. Your whole collection goes over, and every place and set-prep arrives as a playlist. Your genres and tags come along too.
  • BPM and key are left for rekordbox to work out itself — it's better at it.
The tour · Settings

The dials

  • Settings holds the practical things: where your music lives, where the Library folder and backups go, how the room looks and sounds.
  • Backups — one button copies the database, artwork and notes to your backup folder. Worth pressing now and then.
  • Nothing you do in here is sent anywhere. It's all on this Mac.
Everything else you'll need day to day — import, analyse, export, recordings, the map — lives under Tools.
Sharpen it (optional)

Free keys that teach it more

Music Room looks things up from free, open sources — MusicBrainz, Discogs, Deezer, iTunes. No AI, no subscriptions. A couple of free keys make it noticeably smarter:

Discogs token — unlocks proper DJ-grade genre suggestions (Deep House, Italo Disco, that level of detail) and better record labels.

discogs.com/settings/developers → “Generate new token”. Free, 30 seconds.

Last.fm key — adds crowd-tagged genres to the suggestions. Optional.

last.fm/api/account/create. Also free.
